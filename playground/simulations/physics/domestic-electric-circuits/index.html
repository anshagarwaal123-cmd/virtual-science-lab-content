Safety devices — what do they protect against?

Compare their roles
FUSE / MCB
Overcurrent protection
Disconnects a circuit when excessive current flows because of an overload or short circuit. A fuse melts; an MCB trips and can be reset after the fault is cleared.
RCCB / RCD
Residual-current protection
Detects a difference between current flowing in live and returning through neutral. It can disconnect a circuit when leakage current exceeds its operating threshold.
EARTH WIRE
Protective earthing
Connects exposed conductive parts to earth so a fault can create a protective fault path. It is not intended to carry normal operating current.

Scientific model note: This is a simplified educational household circuit using an illustrative 230 V AC supply. Appliance current is estimated as P/V, treating listed power ratings as equivalent resistive loads. Real motor loads have power factor and changing current. MCB/fuse operation is simplified to an overcurrent threshold; real devices have time-current characteristics. The leakage demonstration uses an illustrative residual-current value and a simplified RCD response. Actual domestic wiring, protective-device selection and installation must be handled by qualified electricians and follow applicable local standards.
